## Scene 1 — the world as it actually is (Manim)
1. Everyone you date seems to prove it: the more attractive someone is, the meaner they are. *(hook — no visual)*
2. Two axes hold every person you could ever meet.
3. Sideways measures how attractive they are.
4. Upward measures how nice they are.
5. A cloud of dots fills the space evenly, one dot per person.
6. A flat trend line through the cloud says looks and kindness are unrelated.

## Scene 2 — your filters (Manim) [CUT]
7. Here is that cloud again, now as your dating pool.
8. You skip anyone who is neither cute nor kind.
9. The whole bottom corner of the pool vanishes.
10. Meanwhile the people who are both gorgeous and kind are busy skipping you.
11. The golden top corner disappears too.
12. What survives is a diagonal band from nice-but-plain down to hot-but-mean.

## Scene 3 — the reveal (Manim) [CUT]
13. Look at the band by itself.
14. A new trend line through the survivors tilts sharply downward.
15. In your dating pool, hotter now really does mean meaner.
16. But nobody's personality changed — your filters built that slope out of thin air.
17. Statisticians call this trick Berkson's paradox.

## Scene 4 — the kicker (Doodle) [CUT]
18. Google once studied its own programming-contest stars.
19. Among the people Google hired, contest wins predicted worse everyday performance.
20. The hiring bar had already thrown away everyone who was weak at both.
21. Any time a gate picks winners, the survivors show patterns the world never had.
22. So before you trust a pattern, ask who got filtered out. *(HOLD)*
